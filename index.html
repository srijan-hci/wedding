Travel & stay

People are traveling to Bangalore from all over India and from much farther away. We appreciate you making the journey to get here, and we’ve tried to make the rest as easy as possible.

Getting to Bangalore

Fly into Kempegowda International Airport (BLR), Bangalore’s main airport.

The wedding will be at The Tharavadu Mane in Yelahanka, north Bangalore. It’s outside the main city, so give yourself some extra time when traveling to and from the venue: Bangalore traffic is reliably bad and can cause some delays.

Uber is widely used in Bangalore for ridesharing. For the wedding celebrations, we’ll arrange transportation between the main guest hotels and the venue, so you won’t need to figure that part out yourself.

Where to stay

We’re happy to take care of your accommodation near the venue for the nights of February 19, 20, and 21.

If you’d like us to arrange a room for you, just let us know when you RSVP. We’ll share more details about the hotels and rooms as we get closer to the date.

Visas

If you’re traveling to India on a foreign passport, you may need an e-Tourist Visa. You can find more information and apply through the official Indian e-Visa website.

Please apply well in advance. It generally takes roughly two weeks from the time of application to receive your visa, though processing times can vary.

A few practical things

February is one of the nicest times to be in Bangalore, with warm days and cooler evenings. Credit cards are widely accepted, though it’s useful to carry some Indian rupees for smaller purchases. Uber/auto-rickshaws are the easiest ways to get around.

If you have any questions about travel, accommodation, or the wedding, just reach out to us. We’re happy to help with more information or travel ideas for you to spend your time here!

Events & theme

We’re celebrating over two days, and we’d love for you to join us for as much as you can. The final schedule is still coming together and we’ll update this with more details as we go, but here’s the general plan of events and a little bit about what they represent.

Saturday, 20 February

Haldi
This ceremony starts by family & friends covering the bride and groom (us) in a turmeric paste that symbolizes good fortune and new beginnings. It’s a traditional ceremony where you can offer us your blessings. Wearing yellow, orange, and other sunset colors is especially encouraged.
Mehendi
Traditionally, the night before the wedding the bride has mehendi (henna) applied in intricate designs over her hands and feet in preparation of the big day. We think it’s fun to have everyone be a part of the tradition and will also have music, snacks, mehendi artists for all who wish to join in, and plenty of time for the designs to dry.
Sangeet
This will be an evening of music and dancing, with performances from both the families and friends with varying amounts of rehearsal. Eventually, this devolves into a big dance party so keep your dancing shoes on!

Sunday, 21 February

Wedding ceremony
This is the big day: traditional Hindu weddings have a priest guiding the bride and groom through various rituals. We’ll exchange garlands, repeat wedding mantras, and take seven pheras (circles) around the sacred fire, each symbolizing a promise we make to one another as we begin our life together.
Reception
At this point, we’re officially married (wooo!) and the reception is all about celebrating that with dinner, speeches, likely some more dancing, and lots of good food to wrap up all the festivities!

Bangalore & around

If you’re coming all the way to Bangalore, we hope you’ll give yourself a little extra time to explore the city!

We both love the city of Bangalore, with its enormous trees and busy streets, century-old markets and excellent coffee (which as you know Srijan loves), temples and dosa breakfasts, and some of the best restaurants and breweries in the country.

Here are a few places we’d recommend.

For a slow morning

Lalbagh Botanical Garden
One of Bangalore’s best places for a morning walk. It’s huge, green, and a nice way to escape the hustle and bustle of the city for a couple of hours.
Cubbon Park
Another great place for a stroll, especially if you’re staying in central Bangalore. It’s right in the middle of the city but feels serene and peaceful.

For food

South Indian breakfast
Don’t leave Bangalore without having a proper bene dosa, idli, vada, and filter coffee. MTR and CTR are a few of the city’s oldest institutions and a good place to start.
Cafes & breweries
Bangalore has an enormous food scene, from tiny neighborhood spots to some excellent restaurants and cafes (the city is surrounded by coffee plantations)! Indiranagar, MG Road, and Koramangala are good neighborhoods to explore if you’re looking for variety.

Neighborhoods to wander around

Malleswaram
Old Bangalore, with temples, markets, neighborhood restaurants, and lots of good places to stop for a snack.
Church Street & MG Road
More central, more modern, and good for an afternoon or evening of walking around, shopping, coffee, and people-watching.
Indiranagar
One of the city’s livelier neighborhoods, with restaurants, bars, cafés, and good nightlife- ask us if you want recommendations :).

If you have a little more time

Nandi Hills
A popular day trip north of Bangalore for beautiful landscapes and an easy addition if you have a spare morning.
Mysore
If you have an extra day, Mysore makes for a great trip from Bangalore. The palace is spectacular, and the city has a very different pace from Bangalore.

Lastly, if you have any questions or need help to plan your travels, ideas on what to do and where to go, just ask us - Srijan spent a lot of time in Bangalore and has a bunch of recommendations he’s excited to share. We hope you love the city as much as we do!

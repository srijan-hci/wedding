Travel & stay

People are coming to Bangalore from all over India and from much farther afield, so we know getting here is a bit of a journey. We’ve tried to make the rest as easy as possible.

Getting to Bangalore

Fly into Kempegowda International Airport (BLR), Bangalore’s main airport.

The wedding will be at The Tharavadu Mane in Yelahanka, in north Bangalore. It’s outside the main part of the city, so give yourself some extra time when traveling to and from the venue: Bangalore traffic has a way of making its own schedule.

Uber and Ola are widely used in Bangalore. For the wedding celebrations, we’ll arrange transportation between the main guest hotels and the venue, so you won’t need to figure that part out yourself.

Where to stay

We’re happy to take care of your accommodation for the nights of February 19, 20, and 21.

If you’d like us to arrange a room for you, just let us know when you RSVP. We’ll share more details about the hotels and rooms as we get closer.

Visas

If you’re traveling to India on a foreign passport, you may need an e-Tourist Visa. You can find more information and apply through the official Indian e-Visa website.

Please apply well in advance. It generally takes roughly two weeks from the time of application to receive your visa, though processing times can vary.

A few practical things

February is one of the nicest times to be in Bangalore, with warm days and cooler evenings. Cards are widely accepted, though it’s useful to carry some Indian rupees for smaller purchases. Uber and Ola are the easiest ways to get around.

If you have any questions about travel, accommodation, or the wedding, just reach out to Srijan or Akriti. We’re happy to help with more information.

Events & theme

We’re celebrating over two days, and we’d love for you to join us for as much as you can. The final schedule is still coming together, but here’s the general idea.

Saturday, 20 February

Haldi
We’ll start by covering each other in turmeric, with help from pretty much everyone we know. It’s a traditional ceremony where family and friends offer their blessings. Yellow, orange, and other sunset colors are especially encouraged.
Mehendi
Henna, music, snacks, and plenty of time for the designs to dry. Artists will be there, so if you’d like mehendi on yourself, you’re encouraged to join in.
Sangeet
An evening of music and dancing, featuring performances from both families and varying amounts of rehearsal. Eventually, it will become a dance party.

Sunday, 21 February

Wedding ceremony
We’ll get married around a sacred fire, with a priest guiding us through the traditional rituals. The ceremony takes some time, with plenty happening around it, so don’t worry about sitting perfectly still throughout.
Reception
Dinner, speeches, likely some more dancing.

What should I wear?

Come dressed to celebrate.

Indian clothes are very welcome, but you do not need to buy or wear traditional clothing. Western formalwear, including dresses, suits, shirts, and trousers, is completely fine.

Do I need to come to both days?

Yeah, why not? However, if you can only make one day, Sunday, 21 February is the main event, with the wedding ceremony and reception.

What about gifts?

Bangalore & around

If you’re coming all the way to Bangalore, we hope you’ll give yourself a little time to see the city.

We both really like Bangalore, now known as Bengaluru, with its enormous trees and busy streets, century-old markets and excellent coffee, temples and cocktail bars, dosa breakfasts and some of the best restaurants in the country. It’s also a city that rewards wandering around without too much of a plan.

Here are a few places we’d recommend.

For a slow morning

Lalbagh Botanical Garden
One of Bangalore’s best places for a morning walk. It’s huge, green, and a nice way to escape the traffic for a couple of hours.
Cubbon Park
Another great place to walk, especially if you’re staying in central Bangalore. It’s right in the middle of the city but feels remarkably removed from it.

For food

South Indian breakfast
Please don’t leave Bangalore without having a proper dosa, idli, vada, and filter coffee. MTR is one of the city’s old institutions and is a good place to start.
Local food
Bangalore has an enormous food scene, from tiny neighborhood spots to some genuinely excellent restaurants. Indiranagar, MG Road, and Koramangala are good places to explore if you’re looking for variety.

For wandering around

Malleswaram
Old Bangalore, with temples, markets, neighborhood restaurants, and plenty of good places to stop for a snack.
Church Street & MG Road
More central, more modern, and good for an afternoon or evening of walking around, shopping, coffee, and people-watching.
Indiranagar
One of the city’s livelier neighborhoods, with restaurants, bars, cafés, and plenty happening after dark.

If you have a little more time

Nandi Hills
A popular day trip north of Bangalore and an easy addition if you have a spare morning.
Mysore
If you have an extra day, Mysore makes for a great trip from Bangalore. The palace is spectacular, and the city has a very different pace from Bangalore.

One last thing

Don’t feel like you need to turn this trip into a checklist of attractions. Bangalore is a good city to simply spend time in: have breakfast somewhere, find a café, walk around, eat something you’ve never tried, and see where the day takes you.

And if you’re not sure where to go, ask us. We’ll tell you where we’d actually go.

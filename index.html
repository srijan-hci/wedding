The venue

Travel and stay

Getting to Bangalore

Guests are coming from across the United States and from all over India. Here is everything we think you will need. If something is missing, please just ask us.

Flights

Coming from the USA

Fly into Bengaluru (BLR), Kempegowda International Airport. Air India runs a nonstop from San Francisco. From everywhere else the usual one-stop routes are Emirates via Dubai, Qatar Airways via Doha, Etihad via Abu Dhabi, Lufthansa via Frankfurt, British Airways via London, or Air India via Delhi.

Expect 20 to 26 hours door to door from the East Coast, and 18 to 24 from the West Coast. India is 10.5 hours ahead of US Eastern time and 13.5 ahead of Pacific, so give yourself a day to recover before the celebrations start.

Coming from within India

BLR is served by IndiGo, Air India, SpiceJet and Akasa from every major city. By train, the two main stations are KSR Bengaluru (SBC), also called Majestic, which is the main terminal and sits in the city centre, and Yeshwantpur Junction (YPR) in the north, which handles most long-distance trains from Delhi, Mumbai and Kerala.

Yeshwantpur is the more convenient of the two for the venue, at roughly 15 km away.

Visas for guests travelling from outside India

Most visitors, including US passport holders, need an e-Tourist Visa. It is applied for entirely online and approved by email, so there is no need to send your passport anywhere or visit a consulate. Bengaluru is one of the airports that accepts e-Visa arrivals.

Where to apply
indianvisaonline.gov.in/evisa
When to apply
From 120 days before you travel, and no later than 4 days before. Approval usually lands within 72 hours, but allow up to five working days.
Which one
The 30-day double-entry visa covers the wedding comfortably. One-year and five-year multiple-entry options exist if you plan to come back.
Passport
Must be valid for at least six months beyond your arrival date, with two blank pages.

Use the official site only

There are many convincing look-alike websites with names like "indianevisa" or "india-evisa" that charge large extra fees or harvest your passport details. The Indian government is explicit that indianvisaonline.gov.in/evisa is the only official website and that no agent or intermediary is needed. Type the address in by hand rather than trusting a search result.

Getting from the airport into the city

The airport is in Devanahalli, about 40 km north of central Bangalore. Bangalore traffic is genuinely heavy, so add 50 to 100 per cent to any journey time between roughly 7 to 10 am and 5 to 9 pm.

Uber and Ola
Both work well and have a marked pick-up area at arrivals. Around ₹600 to ₹1,500, and 45 to 90 minutes. Easiest option if you already have the apps.
Prepaid taxi
A government counter inside arrivals. You pay a fixed fare up front, so there is nothing to negotiate. Around ₹800 to ₹1,500.
Vayu Vajra airport bus
Air-conditioned government coaches running to Hebbal, Indiranagar, Majestic and other points. Around ₹170 to ₹250. Cheap and reliable, but slower.
Metro
A new metro line to the airport is under construction and may be open by early 2027. We will update this if it is.

Practical things worth knowing

Weather
February is Bangalore at its best. Days around 27°C (81°F), nights around 14°C (58°F), and almost no rain. The city sits at 900 metres, which is why it never gets as hot as the rest of India.
What to pack
Light cottons and linens for the daytime, and a jacket, shawl or cardigan for the evenings, which genuinely do cool off. Comfortable shoes you can slip on and off, as you will take them off often.
Money
Indian rupees. ATMs are everywhere, including in the arrivals hall. Cards work at hotels and most restaurants, but carry some cash for autos, markets and street food.
Phone and data
Jio and Airtel have counters at arrivals. Bring your passport and a copy of your visa. Alternatively, set up an eSIM before you fly. Coverage across Bangalore is excellent.
Plugs
India uses Type D and Type C sockets at 230 volts. Phones and laptops only need a plug adapter, but American hairdryers and stylers usually need a voltage converter or a travel version.
Language
Kannada is the local language and Hindi is widely understood, but English is spoken almost everywhere in Bangalore. You will have no trouble.
Water
Stick to bottled or filtered water. Every hotel and restaurant serves it as a matter of course.
For first-timers

If this is your first Indian wedding

Nobody expects you to know any of this, and nobody will mind if you get something wrong. But people tell us they enjoy it far more when they know roughly what is going on, so here it is.

What actually happens

Haldi

Turmeric paste is applied to the bride and groom by family and friends, as a blessing and, traditionally, to make the skin glow. It stains, so wear something old. Guests join in.

Mehendi

Henna is drawn on the hands and feet in fine patterns. Artists will be on hand and you are very welcome to have some done. It takes an hour or two to set and the colour deepens overnight.

Sangeet

A party. Both families perform dances they have been rehearsing for weeks, with varying success, and then everyone else joins in. No skill required, only willingness.

The ceremony

The rituals take place around a sacred fire, led by a priest, in Sanskrit. It runs long, and guests talk, move about and eat throughout. That is not rudeness, it is simply how it works.

The reception

Dinner and celebration, and the most formal event of the weekend. This is where the two of us finally get to spend proper time with everyone.

What to wear

  • Indian clothes are very welcome but never expected. If you would like to wear them, a kurta for men and a salwar kameez, lehenga or saree for women all work. Plenty of shops in Bangalore will help you, and we are happy to point you to good ones.
  • Western formal is completely fine at every event. Think what you would wear to a smart wedding at home.
  • Colour is the point. Indian weddings are bright, so this is the occasion to wear the loudest thing you own.
  • Avoid pure white or all black for the ceremony. Both carry associations with mourning in Indian tradition.
  • Red is traditionally the bride's colour for the ceremony, so it is kind to leave head-to-toe red to her that day.
  • Wear shoes you can slip off easily. You will be asked to remove them in places, and much of the venue is semi-outdoors.
  • For the Haldi, wear something you genuinely do not mind ruining. Turmeric does not come out.

Small things that help

  • Meals are largely vegetarian and can be spicy. There will always be milder options, and please tell us about any allergies on the RSVP form.
  • Eating with your right hand is normal and cutlery is always available. Nobody is watching.
  • Tipping is around 10 per cent in restaurants. Autos and taxis do not expect it.
  • Bargaining is expected at markets like Commercial Street, but not in shops, malls or restaurants.
  • Ask before photographing people, particularly during religious parts of the ceremony.
  • The emergency number in India is 112.
While you are here

Bangalore and beyond

You have come a long way. It would be a shame to see only the inside of a wedding venue. February is the best month of the year to be in this part of India, so stay a few extra days if you can.

In the city

Green and old

  • Lalbagh Botanical Garden, laid out in 1760, with a glass house modelled on London's Crystal Palace
  • Cubbon Park, 300 acres of trees in the middle of the city, best early in the morning
  • Bangalore Palace, a Tudor-style royal residence built in the 1880s
  • Vidhana Soudha, the granite state legislature building, particularly good lit up at night

Eat

  • Vidyarthi Bhavan in Basavanagudi, making the same crisp masala dosa since 1943
  • Mavalli Tiffin Room (MTR), a Bangalore institution since 1924
  • VV Puram Food Street, an entire lane of South Indian street food, best after dark
  • Bangalore is India's craft beer capital. Toit in Indiranagar and Byg Brewski in Hennur are the ones people talk about.

Browse

  • Commercial Street for clothes, fabric and jewellery, and the best place to have an outfit made
  • Museum of Art and Photography (MAP), five floors and a very good collection
  • Karnataka Chitrakala Parishath for Indian painting and sculpture
  • Indiranagar and Koramangala for the densest concentration of restaurants and bars

Worth leaving the city for

  • Nandi Hills 1.5 hours

    A hilltop fort north of the city, and the classic Bangalore sunrise. Leave very early and you will be above the clouds.

  • Mysore 3 hours

    The city of palaces. Mysore Palace is illuminated on Sunday evenings and is genuinely spectacular. Silk, sandalwood and Chamundi Hills are all worth the trip. Easy as a long day or a comfortable overnight.

  • Coorg 5 hours

    Coffee and pepper plantations in the Western Ghats, misty and cool. Best as a two or three night stay rather than a rush.

  • Chikmagalur 5 hours

    India's coffee country, up at 1,100 metres, with good walking in the Baba Budangiri range.

  • Wayanad 5 hours

    Over the border into Kerala. Forested hills, tea and coffee estates, elephants, and the prehistoric Edakkal Caves.

  • Pondicherry 6 hours

    A former French colony on the east coast, with pastel streets, the Sri Aurobindo Ashram and very good food. Worth a few nights.

  • Hampi 7 hours

    A UNESCO World Heritage site: the ruins of the Vijayanagara empire scattered across a surreal landscape of giant boulders. One of the most extraordinary places in India, and worth the overnight journey.

Travel & stay

Guests are coming from across the United States and from all over India. If something you need is missing here, please just ask us.

Flights

Fly into Bengaluru (BLR), Kempegowda International. Air India runs a nonstop from San Francisco. From everywhere else the usual one-stops are Emirates via Dubai, Qatar via Doha, Etihad via Abu Dhabi, Lufthansa via Frankfurt, British Airways via London, or Air India via Delhi.

Expect 20 to 26 hours door to door from the US East Coast and 18 to 24 from the West. India is 10.5 hours ahead of US Eastern and 13.5 ahead of Pacific, so give yourself a day to recover before things start.

From within India, BLR is served by IndiGo, Air India, SpiceJet and Akasa from every major city.

Visas

Most visitors need an e-Tourist Visa. Apply at indianvisaonline.gov.in/evisa. That is the only official site, so type it in by hand: there are convincing look-alikes that charge large fees for nothing. You can apply from 120 days before you travel, and it is worth doing early.

Getting to the venue

Tharavadu Mane is in north Bangalore, near Nagawara Lake, roughly 30 to 35 km from the airport and about an hour by car outside peak traffic. Uber and Ola cover the area well. We will arrange shuttles from the main guest hotels.

Please do not book a hotel on distance alone yet. We are still waiting on the exact street address from the venue and will put it here the moment we have it.

Where to stay

We will confirm a room block and rates here once they are arranged. In the meantime, north Bangalore keeps you close to the venue and the city centre is better if you want to explore between events.

  • Near the venue: The Leela Bhartiya City on Thanisandra Main Road, Radisson Blu on the Outer Ring Road, Novotel Bengaluru ORR, plus plenty of good budget options in Hebbal, Nagawara and HBR Layout.
  • City centre: The Oberoi and The Ritz-Carlton near MG Road, ITC Gardenia, Taj West End, JW Marriott on Vittal Mallya Road, and Lemon Tree or Ibis for something more modest.

February is peak wedding season in India and hotels fill up, so book early. Rates move a lot: compare Booking.com, MakeMyTrip and the hotel's own site before committing.

Worth knowing

  • Weather. February is Bangalore at its best: around 27°C (81°F) by day and 14°C (57°F) at night. Pack light clothes and something warm for the evenings.
  • Money. Cards are widely accepted, but carry some cash. UPI runs the country and most foreign visitors cannot use it, so do not rely on it.
  • Water. Drink bottled or filtered water. Street food is part of the point of Bangalore, so eat it, just choose stalls with a queue.
  • Phones. An Airtel or Jio tourist SIM at the airport is cheap and makes cabs and maps far easier.

Events & theme

Two days, and you are welcome at all of it. Timings are still being settled and everyone who has replied will get an email once they are.

Saturday 20 February

  • Haldi. Turmeric paste is smeared on both of us by everyone who loves us. It stains, so wear something you do not mind ruining, ideally yellow.
  • Mehendi. Henna drawn on hands and feet, with music and a great deal of sitting around while it dries. Artists will be there for guests too.
  • Sangeet. Music, dancing, and both families performing routines they have rehearsed far too little. This is the loud one.

Sunday 21 February

  • The ceremony. Around a sacred fire, with a priest guiding us through the rituals. It runs long, and guests quite normally talk, move about and eat during it. That is not rudeness, it is how it works.
  • The reception. Dinner, speeches, and dancing until someone turns the lights on.

What to wear

Anything bright and festive. Indian clothes are very welcome but never expected, and Western formal is perfectly appropriate at every event. A few things that help:

  • Avoid pure white and all black for the ceremony.
  • Leave head-to-toe red to the bride.
  • Wear shoes you can slip off easily. You will take them off more than once.
  • For the haldi, wear something you are happy to lose to turmeric.

Do I need to come to both days?

No. Come to whatever you can. If you can only make one, the ceremony and reception on Sunday 21 February are the heart of it.

Can I bring my partner or my children?

Your invitation will say who it covers. Children are genuinely welcome at Indian weddings and there will be plenty of them. If you are unsure, just ask us on the form.

Bangalore & around

You have come a long way. It would be a shame to see only the inside of a wedding venue. February is the best month of the year to be in this part of India, so stay a few extra days if you can.

In the city

  • Lalbagh Botanical Garden, laid out in 1760, with a glass house modelled on London's Crystal Palace.
  • Cubbon Park, 300 acres of trees in the middle of the city. Best early in the morning.
  • Bangalore Palace, a Tudor-style royal residence from the 1880s, and Vidhana Soudha, the granite state legislature, particularly good lit up at night.
  • Museum of Art and Photography (MAP), five floors and a very good collection, and Karnataka Chitrakala Parishath for Indian painting and sculpture.

Eat

  • Vidyarthi Bhavan in Basavanagudi, making the same crisp masala dosa since 1943.
  • Mavalli Tiffin Room (MTR), a Bangalore institution since 1924.
  • VV Puram Food Street, an entire lane of South Indian street food. Best after dark.
  • Bangalore is India's craft beer capital. Toit in Indiranagar and Byg Brewski in Hennur are the ones people talk about.

Browse

  • Commercial Street for clothes, fabric and jewellery, and the best place to have an outfit made.
  • Indiranagar and Koramangala for the densest concentration of restaurants and bars.

Worth leaving the city for

Nandi Hills 1.5 hours
A hilltop fort north of the city, and the classic Bangalore sunrise. Leave very early and you will be above the clouds.
Mysore 3 hours
The city of palaces. Mysore Palace is illuminated on Sunday evenings and is genuinely spectacular. Silk, sandalwood and Chamundi Hills are all worth the trip. Easy as a long day or a comfortable overnight.
Coorg 5 hours
Coffee and pepper plantations in the Western Ghats, misty and cool. Best as a two or three night stay rather than a rush.
Chikmagalur 5 hours
India's coffee country, up at 1,100 metres, with good walking in the Baba Budangiri range.
Wayanad 5 hours
Over the border into Kerala. Forested hills, tea and coffee estates, elephants, and the prehistoric Edakkal Caves.
Pondicherry 6 hours
A former French colony on the east coast, with pastel streets, the Sri Aurobindo Ashram and very good food. Worth a few nights.
Hampi 7 hours
A UNESCO World Heritage site: the ruins of the Vijayanagara empire scattered across a surreal landscape of giant boulders. One of the most extraordinary places in India, and worth the overnight journey.

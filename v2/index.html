Tharavadu Mane, Bangalore

A tharavadu is the ancestral home of a Kerala family: four wings around an open courtyard, tiled roofs, carved wooden pillars. Mane is Kannada for house. It is a semi-outdoor heritage home rather than a banquet hall, which is exactly why we chose it.

Questions

What should I wear?

Anything bright and festive. Indian clothes are very welcome but never expected, and Western formal is perfectly appropriate at every event. Avoid pure white or all black for the ceremony, and leave head-to-toe red to the bride. Wear shoes you can slip off easily.

Do I need to come to both days?

No, come to whatever you can. If you can only make one, the ceremony and reception on Sunday 21 February are the heart of it.

I have never been to an Indian wedding. What happens?

Saturday is haldi, where turmeric is smeared on us by everyone we love, then mehendi, where henna is drawn on hands and feet, then sangeet, which is music and dancing and both families performing routines. Sunday is the ceremony around a sacred fire, which runs long and during which guests quite normally talk, move about and eat, followed by the reception.

I am flying from the USA. What do I need?

Fly into Bengaluru (BLR) and apply for an e-Tourist Visa at indianvisaonline.gov.in/evisa. That is the only official site, so type it in by hand: there are convincing look-alikes that charge large fees. Apply from 120 days before you travel. February in Bangalore is lovely, around 27°C by day and 14°C at night, so pack light clothes and something warm for the evenings.

Can I bring my partner or my children?

Your invitation will say who it covers. Children are genuinely welcome at Indian weddings and there will be plenty of them. If you are unsure, just ask us on the form.
